Ball valves for offshore platform seawater lines

Written by our valve engineering team · Updated March 2026 · 6 min read

Offshore seawater service combines chloride attack with constant vibration — the two failure modes that kill a valve fastest, and the two that datasheets rarely address together.

The 316L body resists pitting that would perforate a 304 valve within a season. Molybdenum content is what makes the difference: at 2–3%, it stabilises the passive layer against chloride ingress in a way 304 simply cannot. On a platform where a leaking valve means a shutdown, that margin is the whole argument.

Why two-piece construction matters here

The two-piece bolted construction allows seat replacement in place rather than cutting the valve out of the line. Offshore, where hot work permits are slow and welding is restricted, being able to service a valve without a permit is worth more than the unit price difference.

Specify the ISO 5211 pad option if the line will later be actuated for remote shutdown. Retrofitting a mounting pad offshore is not practical.

Specification checklist

  • 316L body and ball, not 304 — confirm with a 3.1 material certificate
  • PTFE or RPTFE seat rated for the line's maximum temperature
  • ISO 5211 pad sized for the actuator you may fit later
  • Anti-static device between ball, stem and body
