## The demand

Aerospace missions demand relentless strength-to-weight ratios and 100% metallurgical traceability. A fastener on a launch vehicle or in a defense avionics bay has to be light, resistant to fatigue and traceable to its heat lot, and it works under three conditions at once:

- **Thermal excursions** from -150°C in shadow to +350°C in atmospheric heating.
- **Acoustic vibration** that is severe, with random load spectra up to 32g RMS.
- **Launch mass cost penalties** estimated at $5,000–$15,000 per kg, so every gram in a joint is paid for at liftoff.

## Why titanium here

Our Grade 5 (Ti-6Al-4V, AMS 4928) titanium fasteners replace stainless steel bolts in satellite truss assemblies, engine cowl mounts and payload bay separation mechanisms. The change delivers 45% weight savings with zero risk of stress-corrosion cracking.

Grade 5 is the benchmark alloy for high-stress fasteners where strength-to-weight ratio and fatigue life come first. For this sector we recommend:

- Grade 5 (Ti-6Al-4V, AMS 4928)
- Grade 23 ELI (AMS 4930)
- Custom age-hardened alloys

Composition and mechanical data for each grade are in the [material guide](/material-guide). For a worked example, see our case study on [orbital stage separation fasteners](/case-studies/orbital-stage-separation-fasteners).
