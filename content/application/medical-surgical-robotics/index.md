## The demand

Modern surgical robotics and intraoperative MRI suites require non-ferromagnetic hardware with total chemical bio-inertness. A screw in a robotic joint or a surgical instrument is held to three requirements:

- **No magnetic signature.** Absolute zero ferromagnetic distortion in 3T and 7T magnetic resonance fields.
- **Sterilization.** Resistance to aggressive enzymatic detergents and high-pressure steam autoclaves.
- **Miniature precision.** Micro-screw dimensional tolerances down to ±0.005 mm on miniature threads.

## Why titanium here

Our fasteners for this sector are degreased in a Class 10,000 cleanroom. They generate zero magnetic susceptibility artifacts and endure thousands of autoclave steam sterilization cycles without surface oxidation. Stainless steel 316L, by comparison, turns slightly magnetic when it is worked.

We recommend two grades:

- **Grade 23 ELI**, the extra low interstitial form of Ti-6Al-4V, selected where strict surgical biocompatibility is mandatory. Its oxygen content is held to 0.13% or less.
- **Grade 2 commercially pure** (ASTM F67), which is unalloyed titanium: at least 99.2% titanium by weight.

Composition and mechanical data for both are in the [material guide](/material-guide). For a worked example, see our case study on [MRI-compatible surgical robotics](/case-studies/mri-compatible-surgical-robotics).
