## The demand

Austenitic and duplex stainless steels suffer chloride stress pitting, crevice corrosion and hydrogen embrittlement in warm oceanic environments. A fastener that stays immersed for years is exposed to all of that, and to more:

- **Crevice corrosion** beneath screw heads, in oxygen-depleted stagnant seawater.
- **Hydrostatic pressure** exceeding 600 bar at 6,000-meter abyssal depths.
- **Galvanic compatibility** with high-grade carbon-fiber composite submarine hulls.

## Why titanium here

Titanium develops a self-healing crystalline TiO2 passive oxide film. Autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) and offshore acoustic sensors fitted with our titanium fasteners achieve decades of maintenance-free immersion at depth.

We recommend three grades:

- **Grade 2** (ASTM B348), where corrosion immunity in harsh marine brine takes priority over ultra-high tensile clamping strength.
- **Grade 5** (Ti-6Al-4V), for high-stress joints.
- **Grade 7**, enhanced with 0.15% palladium, for preventing crevice corrosion.

Paired with carbon fiber composite, titanium avoids the galvanic oxidation that makes steel or aluminum corrode rapidly beside it. Paired directly with aluminum in maritime environments, titanium can act as a cathode, so we recommend anodization or isolation washers.
