A hex head screw has an external hexagonal head that is turned from outside with a wrench or a socket. It is the most familiar bolt head, and it accepts a high tightening torque because the tool grips the full width of the head.

Engineers choose it for structural joints with room around the head: frames, flanges, mounts, chassis and housings. A flange version adds a circular collar under the hex. The collar spreads the clamp load as a washer would and removes a loose part from the assembly.

## What to check when specifying

- Wrench or socket clearance around the head, including the swing of the tool.
- Fully or partially threaded. A plain shank is preferred where the bolt crosses the joint line in shear.
- Whether a flange is wanted in place of a separate washer.
- Thread size and pitch, and the length measured from under the head.
- Material grade and finish for the load and the environment. The [material guide](/material-guide) compares the titanium grades.
- Thread lubrication and tightening torque, since titanium threads tend to gall when assembled dry.
