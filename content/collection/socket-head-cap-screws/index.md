A socket head cap screw has a cylindrical head with a hexagonal recess in its top face. The tool enters along the axis of the screw, so nothing has to reach the sides of the head. The head can therefore sit in a counterbore, below the surface, or close beside a wall or a neighboring fastener, in places where an external hex could not be turned.

Engineers choose this head for compact, highly loaded joints: brackets, machine frames, housings, tooling and precision assemblies. The tall head and deep socket accept a high tightening torque for the thread size, which makes it the usual default for a structural machine screw.

## What to check when specifying

- Thread size and pitch, and the length measured from under the head.
- Fully or partially threaded, depending on whether a plain shank is wanted in the joint.
- Head diameter and height against the counterbore or the clearance available.
- Access for the hex key along the screw axis.
- Material grade and finish for the load and the environment. The [material guide](/material-guide) compares the titanium grades.
- Thread lubrication and tightening torque, since titanium threads tend to gall when assembled dry.
