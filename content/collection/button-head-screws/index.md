A button head screw has a low, domed head with a recessed drive, usually a hex socket or a Torx. The head stands only slightly above the surface and has no sharp corners, so it does not snag and it leaves a finished appearance.

Engineers choose it for covers, panels, guards, enclosures and bodywork, where a tall cap head would be in the way or exposed to airflow, cables or hands. Because the head is shallow, the drive recess is shallow too. A button head is not the head to pick when a joint needs the highest clamp load: a socket head cap screw of the same thread accepts more torque.

## What to check when specifying

- Drive type and size, and whether a Torx recess is preferred for torque transfer.
- Head diameter and height against the space available.
- The bearing area under the head, especially on soft or composite parts.
- Thread size and pitch, and the length measured from under the head.
- Material grade and finish for the load and the environment. The [material guide](/material-guide) compares the titanium grades.
- The tightening torque the recess allows, not only the torque the thread allows.
