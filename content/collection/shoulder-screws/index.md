A shoulder screw has a head, a precise unthreaded shoulder, and a thread that is smaller in diameter than the shoulder. When the screw is tightened, the end of the shoulder seats against the part. The shoulder length then sets a fixed distance, and the shoulder itself becomes a pin.

Engineers choose it as a removable pivot, axle, guide or locating pin, in linkages, rollers, sliding plates and articulating joints. The moving part rides on the shoulder, not on the thread, so the fit between shoulder and bore decides how the joint behaves.

## What to check when specifying

- Shoulder diameter and its tolerance, against the bore or bearing that runs on it.
- Shoulder length, which sets the clearance for the moving part.
- Thread size and pitch, and the depth of the tapped hole.
- Head and drive style, and the space above the head.
- Surface finish of the shoulder where a part rotates or slides on it.
- Material for the shear load, wear and environment. The [material guide](/material-guide) compares the titanium grades.
