faq:
  - question: "Why is Grade 5 (Ti-6Al-4V) preferred over Grade 2 commercially pure titanium for fasteners?"
    answer: "Grade 5 titanium is an alpha-beta alloy containing 6% aluminum and 4% vanadium. Its yield strength is 828 to 920 MPa, more than double that of commercially pure Grade 2 (275 to 380 MPa). This high-tensile capacity makes Grade 5 the standard for critical stress junctions, high-g components and aerospace structural frames."
  - question: "Which titanium grades do you support?"
    answer: "We work primarily with Grade 5 titanium (Ti-6Al-4V) and commercially pure Grade 2 titanium. Grade 7 palladium titanium alloy, Grade 23 (Ti-6Al-4V ELI) and special aerospace alloys can be specified for parts made to order."
  - question: "Does titanium suffer from galvanic corrosion when coupled with carbon fiber or aluminum?"
    answer: "Titanium is highly noble and does not corrode. When paired with carbon fiber composite, it has identical electrochemical potentials, avoiding galvanic oxidation (unlike steel or aluminum, which corrode rapidly next to carbon fiber). However, when paired directly with aluminum in maritime environments, titanium can act as a cathode, so we recommend anodization or isolation washers."

## Weight savings estimation formula

Grade 5 titanium has a density of 4.43 g/cm³. Replacing a steel fastener with a Grade 5 titanium fastener of the same geometry reduces its mass by:

- `reduction (%) = (steel density − 4.43) ÷ steel density × 100`

That is 43.6% against carbon steel at 7.85 g/cm³ and 44.6% against stainless steel 316 at 8.00 g/cm³. The [fastener calculators](/tools/fastener-calculators) give the mass saved per fastener and per assembly, and the [material guide](/material-guide) compares the grades.
