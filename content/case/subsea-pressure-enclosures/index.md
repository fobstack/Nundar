## The challenge

A manufacturer of autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) systems closed its titanium pressure housings with 316L marine stainless fasteners. In high-salinity deep-sea trenches, under 300 bar of hydrostatic pressure, those fasteners developed aggressive crevice pitting and galling. Threads seized, and expensive titanium housing lids were destroyed.

The task was to replace the 316L marine bolts with a fastener that would prevent deep-ocean crevice corrosion.

## What we made

We supplied flush countersunk socket screws in Grade 2 commercially pure and Grade 5 titanium. Each screw was ultrasonically cleaned, passivated in a nitric acid bath per ASTM A967, and given an anti-galling dry-film treatment on its precision thread.

The two treatments answer the two failures. Passivation leaves a chemically inert titanium dioxide (TiO2) skin on the screw, the film that makes titanium resist seawater where 316L is prone to crevice corrosion. The dry film keeps a titanium thread from galling in a titanium housing, so a lid can be opened again after a long deployment.

The specification, as verified:

- Grade 5 Ti-6Al-4V and Grade 2 commercially pure titanium
- 90° precision countersunk head
- Anti-galling solid film treatment
- Passivation to ASTM A967

The countersunk screw in our catalog is the [M6 × 1.0 titanium countersunk flat screw](/products/titanium-countersunk-flat-screw-m6): 16 mm long (TI-CSK-M6-16), in Grade 2 titanium with a natural finish. The screws for these enclosures share its flush head form but are not that catalog part. They were made to order, in Grade 5 as well as Grade 2 and with the passivation and dry-film treatments described above, through [custom manufacturing](/custom-manufacturing).

## The outcome

- **Certified at 300 bar**, the hydrostatic pressure of the 3,000 m working depth.
- **Zero galvanic attack** between the fasteners and the titanium vessel.
- **24 months deployed without seizing.**
- **100% sealing integrity maintained.**
